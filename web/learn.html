Guide 1 of 6

Build a shared brief

A brief gives you and your client one agreed starting point, so every version that follows answers the same question.

More on shared briefs
  1. Open the project and choose Brief.
  2. Write the idea, the requirements and anything that must stay fixed, then choose Save brief. Each save keeps a revision, so you can see how the direction developed.
  3. Under Creative work, add your visual direction to References so the mood sits beside the words.
  4. Invite your client to the project so they can read the brief and answer questions in Messages.
  5. If the brief changes after you have saved a design draft, Caleane flags the draft so nothing is shared against an old brief.
Guide 2 of 6

Review a design

Feedback is easier to act on when it points at the exact version and the exact detail the client means.

More on design reviews
  1. Under Creative work, open Versions and add a new version of the work. Drafts stay with the studio until you share them.
  2. When it is ready, choose Share for review.
  3. Your client opens that version and pins each comment to the part of the design it is about.
  4. Reply beside each comment, and choose Mark resolved once it has been handled.
  5. Share the next version when you are ready. Earlier versions keep their comments, so the history stays with the project.
Guide 3 of 6

Record a decision

A decision belongs with the version the client actually saw, along with who made it and when.

More on approvals and decisions
  1. A client with the Approver role opens the shared version.
  2. They choose Approve this direction, or Request changes with a note on what should change.
  3. Caleane saves the answer against that version with their name and the date, under the project’s Decisions.
  4. Approvals in the sidebar lists everything still waiting for an answer across your projects.
  5. If changes were requested, share a new version and ask again. Both decisions stay on record.
Guide 4 of 6

Work with a client

Follow one relationship from the first enquiry to the final invoice without switching tools.

More on client relationships
  1. Build an enquiry form in Forms and put it on your website or share it as a link. New enquiries arrive in Pipeline.
  2. Send a quote, hold the date and take a deposit. The enquiry moves along as each step is done, and a paid deposit marks it as won.
  3. The client and their project then sit in Clients and Projects, with the estimate, proposal, contract and invoices under Documents & money.
  4. Keep the conversation in Messages, which shows whether each thread is waiting on you or on them.
Guide 5 of 6

Understand project access

Each person sees what their role needs, and nothing you are still preparing.

More on trust and access
  1. From the project, choose Invite client. Caleane makes a private invitation link that expires, for you to send them.
  2. Pick their role. A Viewer can read the work you share. A Collaborator can also reply and give feedback.
  3. An Approver can also approve versions or request changes, and sees the project’s contract, documents and billing.
  4. Your own team sees the projects you add them to. A bookkeeper sees finances only.
  5. Drafts stay with the studio until you share them, whatever role a client has.

Open the sample workspaceTour the screens first

  3. Open Amelia’s wedding, read the brief, then go to Versions and pin a comment.
  4. Switch to the client view to see the same project as Amelia does, and try approving the direction.
