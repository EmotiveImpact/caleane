# Audience mock review

Reviewed locally on 1 October 2026 at 1280 × 720. Visual review of audience-mock.html; no website edits or deployment. Screenshots below are from this review. This does not certify signup, backend behaviour, accessibility compliance, or every scroll transition.

## Assessment

The direction works. The new client meeting and production photography broaden the audience convincingly. Remaining work is product specificity, image consistency and page pacing.

## Walkthrough

1. **Hero — strong.** Preserve the original portrait, restrained header and clear CTA hierarchy. At this viewport the CTA sits close to the bottom; avoid moving the copy further down. [Screenshot](hero.png)
2. **Problem introduction — coherent.** The large statement and outlined film button fit the identity. Keep the agreed motion treatment; this capture alone does not validate its timing. [Screenshot](why.png)
3. **Product review — useful, content needs refinement.** A tangible interface gives the page substance. The reference thumbnails should all belong to the same wedding design: reception layout, flowers, linen, palette and lighting. Mixed scene imagery weakens the sample's credibility. [Overview](product.png) · [Controls](product-controls.png)
4. **Workflow strip — clear but repetitive.** Brief/review/decision already appears in the demo, followed by three large story scenes. Compress this explanatory repetition to improve pacing. [Screenshot](method.png)
5. **Relationship — keep.** The new image reads as a customer and studio discussing a project. It is sharper and more purposeful. [Screenshot](relationship.png)
6. **Shared brief — weakest photographic match.** The softer living-room image feels less precise than the new meeting photo. Replace with an interiors designer and client reviewing a measured plan and materials at a work table or on site. Compose with a clear left text area. [Screenshot](brief.png)
7. **Decision — stronger occupational variety.** The production setting visibly introduces another kind of creative work. Keep the scene and check final crops across breakpoints before integrating. [Screenshot](decision.png)
8. **Professions — useful breadth.** The animated list communicates the wider audience. Brand/graphic designers and brand/campaign teams overlap; make each category distinct. [Screenshot](professions.png)
9. **Studio examples — crop needs attention.** The narrow brand card cuts off a participant and weakens the customer–company interaction. Use a composition intended for this card or give it more width. Captions are more specific than the earlier general copy. [Images](studios.png) · [Captions](gallery-copy.png)
10. **Access example — change its project.** Repeating Amelia's Wedding reinforces an events-first product despite the broader photographs. Show a packaging or brand-identity project here, with relevant versions and client feedback. [Screenshot](access.png)
11. **Closing — appropriate.** The working team supports the broader studio positioning. [Screenshot](closing.png)
12. **Footer — proportion improved.** The smaller logo fits. Small muted labels could be easier to read. The fixed HTML-mock bar overlaps content and must be removed when integrating the concept. [Screenshot](footer.png)

## Recommended next pass, in order

1. Replace the interiors photograph and correct the brand-card composition.
2. Make the access example a brand project and make wedding reference thumbnails internally consistent.
3. Shorten repeated workflow explanation while retaining the requested scroll scenes.
4. Check final image crops and small-text readability on mobile; remove mock-only controls on integration.

The site still leans toward premium, visually led studios. That is a coherent audience choice. Its product examples should demonstrate the same breadth now present in the photographs.
